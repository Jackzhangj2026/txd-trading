PP Hollow Sheet Thickness Guide: 2mm to 12mm Explained

Published: June 12, 2026 | Reading time: 5 minutes

Introduction

For international buyers sourcing PP hollow sheets — also known as corrugated plastic, fluted polypropylene, or Correx — one of the first and most critical decisions is selecting the right thickness. With options ranging from 2mm ultra-light sheets to 12mm heavy-duty industrial panels, choosing incorrectly can lead to either unnecessary material expense or, worse, packaging failure during transit.

This guide provides a practical, procurement-focused framework for thickness selection. Rather than simply describing each gauge in isolation, we compare them across the metrics that matter most to importers: cost per square meter, weight-to-strength ratio, typical cycle life in returnable systems, and compatibility with common converting processes such as die-cutting, folding, and printing.

Cost-Per-Use Analysis: Why 4mm Is the Sweet Spot

The most common mistake buyers make is focusing on the upfront unit price rather than the cost per use cycle. For returnable packaging applications, the economics change dramatically when you account for trip life:

A 3mm sheet at USD 1.30 per sheet (relative cost 1.3x) lasting 5 trips costs USD 0.26 per trip. A 4mm sheet at USD 1.70 per sheet lasting 20 trips costs just USD 0.085 per trip — a 67% reduction in per-use cost. At 6mm, the per-trip cost drops further to roughly USD 0.05 per use, assuming 50 cycles.

This analysis shows that for any closed-loop logistics system where packaging will be returned and reused, the thicker gauge practically pays for itself within the first few cycles. The breakeven point between 4mm and 3mm typically occurs between trips 8 and 12. For applications exceeding 15 planned trips, 4mm is almost always the most cost-effective choice.

For single-use or limited-use scenarios (e-commerce, one-way export packaging), the equation flips. Here, the upfront cost dominates, and thinner gauges (2mm-3mm) are the rational choice.

Freight Cost Implications by Thickness

Weight directly affects shipping costs, and different thicknesses translate into very different per-container payloads. A standard 20-foot container can hold approximately:

  • 3mm sheets: ~18,000 sheets (based on 0.6m x 0.8m size, flat-packed) — total sheet weight ~6.8 metric tons
  • 4mm sheets: ~14,000 sheets — total weight ~7.0 metric tons
  • 6mm sheets: ~9,000 sheets — total weight ~6.8 metric tons
  • 8mm sheets: ~7,000 sheets — total weight ~7.0 metric tons

Notably, the total freight weight remains relatively stable across thicknesses because as sheets get heavier, fewer fit in the same container volume. The key insight is that ocean freight cost per sheet increases roughly proportionally with thickness. For air freight, where weight is the dominant cost factor, thinner sheets have a more significant advantage — the air freight cost for 3mm sheets is approximately half that of 6mm sheets on a per-sheet basis.

For buyers weighing thickness options, TXD CO., LTD recommends calculating total landed cost (FOB price + freight + duties + inland transport) for each candidate thickness before making a final decision. The difference between 4mm and 5mm may be only 20% in FOB price but can reach 30-35% in total landed cost depending on shipping mode and destination.

Converting and Fabricating: Thickness Matters for Processing

Thickness selection also determines what converting processes can be applied. International buyers who need custom box fabrication should consider these constraints:

Die-cutting: Sheets up to 5mm can be rotary die-cut at high speed. For 6mm and above, flatbed die-cutting is required, which adds 10-15% to fabrication costs. Sheets of 10mm and above often require CNC routing or waterjet cutting for precision work.

V-grooving and folding: Clean V-grooves for 90-degree folding can be reliably produced in sheets from 3mm to 8mm. At 2mm, V-grooving is generally unnecessary as the sheet can be crease-folded. At 10mm and above, deep V-grooving requires specialized tooling and may weaken the fold line.

Printing: Thinner sheets (2mm-4mm) feed more reliably through flexographic and digital printers. For 5mm and above, printing quality remains excellent on the flat surface, but the increased sheet weight requires heavier-duty printing equipment. Screen printing and pad printing are unaffected by thickness.

Welding and joining: Ultrasonic welding works well for sheets up to 5mm. For thicker panels, hot-plate welding or mechanical fastening (rivets, bolts) is recommended for permanent assemblies.

A Decision Framework for First-Time Buyers

For importers who are new to PP hollow sheets, TXD CO., LTD recommends this step-by-step approach:

  1. Define the load: What is the maximum weight your packaging must support, including stacking loads during warehouse storage? Measure the heaviest realistic scenario, not the average.
  2. Count the trips: Is this a one-way shipment, or will the packaging return to you? For 1-5 trips, consider 2mm-3mm. For 5-20 trips, start with 4mm. For 20+ trips, evaluate 5mm or 6mm.
  3. Check the environment: Will the packaging face moisture, extreme temperatures, or UV exposure? Thicker sheets offer more margin against environmental degradation, and UV-stabilized grades should be specified for outdoor or high-humidity use.
  4. Calculate total landed cost: Request quotes for 2-3 thickness options and compare total cost including freight, duties, and fabrication. The optimal choice often becomes clear once you see the full numbers.
  5. Request samples: Before committing to a full container order, ask your supplier for A4-sized samples of your candidate thicknesses. Conduct a simple load test — stack boxes to your expected warehouse height and measure deflection after 24 hours.

Regional Preferences and Market Trends

Different markets show distinct thickness preferences. In European automotive supply chains, 5mm and 6mm dominate due to the rigorous cycle-life requirements of just-in-sequence logistics. North American buyers tend toward 4mm as a general-purpose gauge, reserving 6mm for heavy-duty industrial applications. In Southeast Asian and Middle Eastern markets, 3mm and 4mm are most common, driven by cost sensitivity and shorter logistics chains. The Chinese domestic market, the world's largest producer of PP hollow sheets, uses all thicknesses extensively but shows a marked preference for 4mm in the fast-growing e-commerce logistics sector.

An emerging global trend is the gradual shift toward lighter gauges across all regions, driven by rising freight costs and sustainability targets. Manufacturers are responding with improved material formulations that deliver higher stiffness at the same thickness, allowing buyers to down-gauge from 5mm to 4mm or 4mm to 3mm without sacrificing performance.
